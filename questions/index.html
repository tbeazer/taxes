Questions Every Taxpayer Has to Ask

The questions to work through before preparing a return, grouped by situation instead of by form. Open a section, answer the questions that apply to you, then follow the form links to see where each answer goes on Form 1040 and what limits apply.

Amounts are for tax year 2025. Dollar amounts and dates in the questions use 2025 figures. Pick another year below and the form links open that year's map, which has its own limits.

These questions line up with the IRS's own intake sheet, Form 13614-C, used at free VITA and TCE sites; each section shows which page of it matches. See Free help & resources for the sheet, the volunteer guides and free filing options.

Your household

Who are you, and who lives with you?

25 questions▾

Your marital status on the last day of the year, your age, and who shares your home decide your filing status, your standard deduction, and several deductions and credits.

Marriage & home

  • Were you married on December 31? Divorced or legally separated during the year?Head of householdStandard deduction
  • If married, will you and your spouse file one joint return or two separate ones?Standard deductionHead of household
  • If you're married but filing separately, did your spouse live in your home at any time during the last six months of the year?Head of household
  • Did your spouse die this year or in the last two years, and do you have a dependent child living with you?Standard deduction
  • Who else lived in your home, and for how much of the year?Head of householdDependents
  • Who paid the rent or mortgage, utilities, repairs and food for the home? Did you pay more than half?Head of household
  • Can anyone else claim you as a dependent?Standard deductionDependents

Children & dependents

Who can you claim?

16 questions▾

Each person you support may make you eligible for a credit or a better filing status, but only if they pass every test, and only one person can claim them.

Who qualifies

  • How is each person related to you?Dependents
  • How old was each child at the end of the year? Was any child a full-time student, or permanently and totally disabled?DependentsSchedule 8812Schedule EIC
  • How many nights did each child live with you? What records show it, such as school, medical or daycare records?DependentsSchedule EICSchedule 8812
  • Did any child or relative pay more than half of their own support?Dependents
  • Does each person have a Social Security number that's valid for work?Schedule 8812Schedule EIC
  • Could anyone else, such as the other parent or a grandparent, claim the same child?DependentsSchedule EICSchedule 8812
  • Are you divorced or separated from the child's other parent? Is there a signed Form 8332?Form 8332Schedule 8812
  • For a parent or other relative you support: what was their gross income, and did you pay more than half of their support?DependentsHead of household
  • Is any dependent married and filing a joint return?Dependents
  • Is any dependent in college? See School & education for the credits and the student age rules.Form 8863Dependents

Credits to check

  • Is each child under 17 at the end of the year with a valid Social Security number? Each can bring a child tax credit of up to $2,200.Schedule 8812
  • Is your tax too low to use the whole child tax credit? The additional child tax credit can refund up to $1,700 per child, based on earned income over $2,500.Schedule 8812
  • Do you support a dependent who doesn't qualify for the child tax credit, such as a 17-year-old, a college student or a parent? Each can bring the $500 credit for other dependents.Schedule 8812Dependents
  • Do you have earned income and a qualifying child? The earned income credit is much larger with children.Schedule EIC
  • Did you pay for care so you could work while a child was under 13?Form 2441
  • Did you adopt a child or pay adoption expenses? Part of the adoption credit is refundable starting in 2025.Form 8839

Work & business

How did you earn money?

12 questions▾

Pay from a job comes on a W-2. Work you did for yourself is self-employment, with its own forms and tax.

Your income

  • Do you have a W-2 from every job you had during the year, including part-time and short jobs?W-2
  • Did you receive tips? Were they reported to your employer?W-2Schedule 1-A
  • Did you work overtime that was paid at a higher rate?W-2Schedule 1-A
  • Did you do freelance, gig or contract work, or get a 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC or 1099-K?1099-NEC / MISCSchedule C
  • If you're self-employed, what were your expenses, and do you have records for both the income and the expenses?Schedule CSchedule SE
  • Did you use your car or part of your home for the business?Schedule C
  • Are you an Armed Forces reservist, a performing artist, or a fee-basis government official with unreimbursed work expenses?Form 2106Schedule 1
  • Are you a K–12 teacher or aide who bought classroom supplies?Schedule 1

Credits to check

  • Is your earned income low to moderate? Check the earned income credit, with or without children.Schedule EIC
  • Did you work for two or more employers? If together they withheld more than $10,918.20 of Social Security tax, the extra comes back as a credit.Excess Social SecurityW-2
  • Did you contribute to a retirement plan at work? With a low or moderate income, the saver's credit can add to the tax savings.Form 8880
  • Did you pay for child care so you could work?Form 2441

Retirement

Did you save for retirement or take money out?

10 questions▾

Putting money in can lower your tax or earn a credit. Taking money out is usually taxable, and early withdrawals can cost an extra 10%.

Contributions & withdrawals

  • Did you contribute to a traditional or Roth IRA? Are you or your spouse covered by a retirement plan at work?IRA deductionW-2
  • Did you have earned income to base the IRA contribution on?IRA deduction
  • Have you ever made a nondeductible IRA contribution, or converted to a Roth?Form 8606
  • Did you contribute to a 401(k) or IRA and have a low or moderate income?Form 8880
  • Did you take money out of an IRA, 401(k) or pension? How old were you, and what was the money used for?1099-RForm 5329
  • Was the money rolled over into another retirement account within 60 days?1099-R
  • If you receive a pension, how old were you when payments started, and how much did you pay into the plan with after-tax money?1099-R

Your home

Do you own or rent your home?

8 questions▾

Homeowners may deduct mortgage interest and property taxes if they itemize. Energy improvements and rentals have their own forms.

  • Did you buy a home or refinance this year, and did you pay points?1098

School & education

Did anyone in your household go to school?

12 questions▾

College costs can earn a credit, and student loan interest can be an adjustment to income.

The student

  • Did you, your spouse or a dependent attend college or a trade school? Do you have Form 1098-T?Form 8863
  • Is this one of the student's first four years of college? Were they enrolled at least half-time?Form 8863
  • What did you pay for tuition, fees, books and required materials? Were any costs paid with tax-free scholarships?Form 8863
  • Did you pay interest on a student loan?1098-E
  • Is the student someone else's dependent?Form 88631098-EDependents
  • Is the student your dependent? Only the person who claims the student can take the education credit for them, and a full-time student under 24 can still be your qualifying child.DependentsForm 8863

Credits to check

  • Is the student in the first four years of college, enrolled at least half-time, and not claimed for the AOTC in four earlier years? The American opportunity credit is up to $2,500, and 40% of it (up to $1,000) is refundable.Form 8863
  • Did the student receive a Pell grant or other scholarship? Tax-free grants reduce the expenses you can claim. If the grant's terms allow it to be used for room and board, the student can choose to include part of it in income, which frees up tuition to claim the AOTC. Compare the extra tax with the larger credit.Form 8863
  • Is the student past the first four years, part-time, or taking courses to improve job skills? The lifetime learning credit is 20% of up to $10,000 of expenses, with no limit on the number of years.Form 8863
  • Are you claiming both credits? You can't claim the AOTC and the lifetime learning credit for the same student in the same year.Form 8863
  • Are you the student, under 24, and claiming the AOTC on your own return? The refundable part may not be available to you.Form 8863Dependents
  • Is your modified AGI between $80,000 and $90,000 ($160,000 and $180,000 joint)? Both education credits shrink in that range and stop above it.Form 8863

Paid preparers

What does the IRS require of you?

4 questions▾

If you prepare returns for pay and a return claims the earned income credit, the child tax credit or credit for other dependents, the American opportunity credit, or head of household status, you must meet four due diligence requirements. Each failure can cost a penalty: $650 for 2025 returns ($635 for 2024 returns, $665 for 2026 returns).

  • Complete and submit Form 8867 with the return, based on information the taxpayer gave you.Form 8867
  • Compute the credits correctly, and keep the worksheets that show how.Schedule EICSchedule 8812Form 8863
  • Meet the knowledge requirement: interview the taxpayer, and ask more questions when anything seems incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete.Form 8867Head of householdDependents
  • Keep records of the checklist, worksheets, the questions you asked and the answers, and any documents you reviewed, for 3 years.Form 8867
