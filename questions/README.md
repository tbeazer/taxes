# Questions Every Taxpayer Has to Ask

The questions to work through before preparing a return, grouped by situation instead of by form. Each question lists the forms it leads to; the links open that form's card on the [2025 Form 1040 map](../form-1040/2025/), which shows the 1040 line, the 2025 limits and links to the official IRS form. Dollar amounts and dates in the questions are for tax year 2025; the [2024](../form-1040/2024/) and [2026](../form-1040/2026/) maps have those years' figures.

Browse them as a searchable page: [questions/index.html](index.html).

## Your household: Who are you, and who lives with you?

Your marital status on the last day of the year, your age, and who shares your home decide your filing status, your standard deduction, and several deductions and credits.


### Marriage & home

- Were you married on December 31? Divorced or legally separated during the year?  
  Forms: [Head of household](../form-1040/2025/index.html#hoh), [Standard deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#std)
- If married, will you and your spouse file one joint return or two separate ones?  
  Forms: [Standard deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#std), [Head of household](../form-1040/2025/index.html#hoh)
- If you're married but filing separately, did your spouse live in your home at any time during the last six months of the year?  
  Forms: [Head of household](../form-1040/2025/index.html#hoh)
- Did your spouse die this year or in the last two years, and do you have a dependent child living with you?  
  Forms: [Standard deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#std)
- Who else lived in your home, and for how much of the year?  
  Forms: [Head of household](../form-1040/2025/index.html#hoh), [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)
- Who paid the rent or mortgage, utilities, repairs and food for the home? Did you pay more than half?  
  Forms: [Head of household](../form-1040/2025/index.html#hoh)
- Can anyone else claim you as a dependent?  
  Forms: [Standard deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#std), [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)

### Age

- Were you or your spouse born before January 2, 1961 (age 65 or older at the end of 2025)? Is either of you blind?  
  Forms: [Standard deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#std), [1040-SR](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1040sr)
- If you're 65 or older, is your modified AGI under $75,000 ($150,000 if filing jointly)? The new senior deduction shrinks above that.  
  Forms: [Schedule 1-A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1a)
- Do you receive Social Security benefits? How much other income do you have? (It decides how much of the benefits is taxable.)  
  Forms: [SSA-1099](../form-1040/2025/index.html#ssa)
- Are Medicare premiums taken out of your Social Security? They can count as medical expenses if you itemize.  
  Forms: [SSA-1099](../form-1040/2025/index.html#ssa), [Schedule A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sa)
- Are you 73 or older? Did you take your required minimum distribution from each IRA and retirement plan?  
  Forms: [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r)
- Are you 70½ or older and did you give money to charity straight from an IRA (a qualified charitable distribution)?  
  Forms: [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r)
- Do you receive a pension? How old were you when the payments started?  
  Forms: [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r)
- Are you 50 or older? You can make larger catch-up contributions to an IRA or 401(k).  
  Forms: [IRA deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#ira), [W-2](../form-1040/2025/index.html#w2)
- Did you take money out of a retirement account before age 59½, or leave a job in the year you turned 55 or later?  
  Forms: [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r), [Form 5329](../form-1040/2025/index.html#5329)
- Are you 65 or older (or retired on permanent disability) with low income? You may qualify for the credit for the elderly or disabled (Schedule R).  
  Forms: [Schedule 3](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s3)
- Are you under 25 or over 64 with no qualifying child? You can't claim the earned income credit.  
  Forms: [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic)
- Are you under 24 and a full-time student, or under 19? Your parents may be able to claim you as a dependent.  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep), [Standard deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#std)

### Identity & IRS history

- Do you, your spouse or a dependent have an Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN) from the IRS? It has to be entered on the return.  
  Forms: [Get an IP PIN](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin)
- Was the earned income credit, child tax credit, American opportunity credit or head of household status disallowed on an earlier return? You may need Form 8862 to claim it again.  
  Forms: [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic), [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812), [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863), [Head of household](../form-1040/2025/index.html#hoh), [Form 8862](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8862.pdf)
- Did you receive a letter or notice from the IRS this year? Bring it with you.  
  Forms: [Understanding your IRS notice](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-irs-notice-or-letter)

### Credits to check

- If you're married filing separately, do you know which credits you lose? You generally can't claim the education credits, and the earned income credit and dependent care credit are limited to spouses who are legally separated or lived apart for the last six months of the year.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863), [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic), [Form 2441](../form-1040/2025/index.html#2441)
- Are you 25 to 64 with low earned income and no qualifying child? You may still qualify for a small earned income credit.  
  Forms: [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic)
- Are you 65 or older, or retired on permanent disability, with low income? Check the credit for the elderly or disabled.  
  Forms: [Schedule R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#schr)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 1: your information, marital status and household.

Terms to know: [Filing status](../definitions/README.md), [Head of household (HOH)](../definitions/README.md), [Considered unmarried](../definitions/README.md), [Qualifying surviving spouse (QSS)](../definitions/README.md), [Standard deduction](../definitions/README.md), [Senior deduction](../definitions/README.md), [Catch-up contribution](../definitions/README.md), [Required minimum distribution (RMD)](../definitions/README.md), [Qualified charitable distribution (QCD)](../definitions/README.md), [Credit for the elderly or disabled](../definitions/README.md), [Credit](../definitions/README.md), [Refundable credit](../definitions/README.md), [Nonrefundable credit](../definitions/README.md), [Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN)](../definitions/README.md), [Form 8862](../definitions/README.md)

## Children & dependents: Who can you claim?

Each person you support may make you eligible for a credit or a better filing status, but only if they pass every test, and only one person can claim them.


### Who qualifies

- How is each person related to you?  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)
- How old was each child at the end of the year? Was any child a full-time student, or permanently and totally disabled?  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep), [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812), [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic)
- How many nights did each child live with you? What records show it, such as school, medical or daycare records?  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep), [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic), [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812)
- Did any child or relative pay more than half of their own support?  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)
- Does each person have a Social Security number that's valid for work?  
  Forms: [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812), [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic)
- Could anyone else, such as the other parent or a grandparent, claim the same child?  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep), [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic), [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812)
- Are you divorced or separated from the child's other parent? Is there a signed Form 8332?  
  Forms: [Form 8332](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8332), [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812)
- For a parent or other relative you support: what was their gross income, and did you pay more than half of their support?  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep), [Head of household](../form-1040/2025/index.html#hoh)
- Is any dependent married and filing a joint return?  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)
- Is any dependent in college? See School & education for the credits and the student age rules.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863), [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)

### Credits to check

- Is each child under 17 at the end of the year with a valid Social Security number? Each can bring a child tax credit of up to $2,200.  
  Forms: [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812)
- Is your tax too low to use the whole child tax credit? The additional child tax credit can refund up to $1,700 per child, based on earned income over $2,500.  
  Forms: [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812)
- Do you support a dependent who doesn't qualify for the child tax credit, such as a 17-year-old, a college student or a parent? Each can bring the $500 credit for other dependents.  
  Forms: [Schedule 8812](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8812), [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)
- Do you have earned income and a qualifying child? The earned income credit is much larger with children.  
  Forms: [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic)
- Did you pay for care so you could work while a child was under 13?  
  Forms: [Form 2441](../form-1040/2025/index.html#2441)
- Did you adopt a child or pay adoption expenses? Part of the adoption credit is refundable starting in 2025.  
  Forms: [Form 8839](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8839)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 1: the table of everyone who lived with you or whom you supported.

Terms to know: [Dependent](../definitions/README.md), [Qualifying child](../definitions/README.md), [Qualifying relative](../definitions/README.md), [Tiebreaker rules](../definitions/README.md), [Custodial parent](../definitions/README.md), [Noncustodial parent](../definitions/README.md), [Child tax credit (CTC)](../definitions/README.md), [Additional child tax credit (ACTC)](../definitions/README.md), [Credit for other dependents (ODC)](../definitions/README.md), [Adoption credit](../definitions/README.md)

## Work & business: How did you earn money?

Pay from a job comes on a W-2. Work you did for yourself is self-employment, with its own forms and tax.


### Your income

- Do you have a W-2 from every job you had during the year, including part-time and short jobs?  
  Forms: [W-2](../form-1040/2025/index.html#w2)
- Did you receive tips? Were they reported to your employer?  
  Forms: [W-2](../form-1040/2025/index.html#w2), [Schedule 1-A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1a)
- Did you work overtime that was paid at a higher rate?  
  Forms: [W-2](../form-1040/2025/index.html#w2), [Schedule 1-A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1a)
- Did you do freelance, gig or contract work, or get a 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC or 1099-K?  
  Forms: [1099-NEC / MISC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099nec), [Schedule C](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sc)
- If you're self-employed, what were your expenses, and do you have records for both the income and the expenses?  
  Forms: [Schedule C](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sc), [Schedule SE](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sse)
- Did you use your car or part of your home for the business?  
  Forms: [Schedule C](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sc)
- Are you an Armed Forces reservist, a performing artist, or a fee-basis government official with unreimbursed work expenses?  
  Forms: [Form 2106](../form-1040/2025/index.html#2106), [Schedule 1](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1)
- Are you a K–12 teacher or aide who bought classroom supplies?  
  Forms: [Schedule 1](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1)

### Credits to check

- Is your earned income low to moderate? Check the earned income credit, with or without children.  
  Forms: [Schedule EIC](../form-1040/2025/index.html#eic)
- Did you work for two or more employers? If together they withheld more than $10,918.20 of Social Security tax, the extra comes back as a credit.  
  Forms: [Excess Social Security](../form-1040/2025/index.html#excessss), [W-2](../form-1040/2025/index.html#w2)
- Did you contribute to a retirement plan at work? With a low or moderate income, the saver's credit can add to the tax savings.  
  Forms: [Form 8880](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8880)
- Did you pay for child care so you could work?  
  Forms: [Form 2441](../form-1040/2025/index.html#2441)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 2, Income: wages, tips, self-employment and gig work.

Terms to know: [Earned income](../definitions/README.md), [Wages](../definitions/README.md), [Tips](../definitions/README.md), [Self-employment income](../definitions/README.md), [Self-employment tax](../definitions/README.md), [Excess Social Security tax withheld](../definitions/README.md), [Saver's credit](../definitions/README.md)

## Savings & investments: Did your money earn money?

Interest, dividends and sales of investments are reported on 1099 forms and may need extra schedules.


### Your accounts

- Do you have bank, credit union, brokerage or CD accounts that paid interest?  
  Forms: [1099-INT](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099int)
- Did you cash U.S. savings bonds or own Treasury bills?  
  Forms: [1099-INT](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099int)
- Did you own stocks or mutual funds that paid dividends, including dividends that were reinvested?  
  Forms: [1099-DIV](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099div)
- Did you sell stocks, funds, crypto or other investments? Do you know what you originally paid for them?  
  Forms: [Schedule D / 8949](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sd)
- Do you have a capital loss carried over from last year?  
  Forms: [Schedule D / 8949](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sd)
- Did you withdraw a CD early and pay a penalty?  
  Forms: [1099-INT](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099int), [Schedule 1](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1)
- Do you have any bank or investment accounts outside the U.S., or did you pay foreign tax?  
  Forms: [1099-INT](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099int), [Form 1116](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1116)
- Does a child in your home have investment income?  
  Forms: [Form 8615](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8615)
- Did you receive, sell, exchange or otherwise dispose of a digital asset such as cryptocurrency or an NFT? Every return asks this question.  
  Forms: [Schedule D / 8949](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sd), [IRS: Digital assets](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/digital-assets)

### Credits to check

- Does your 1099-DIV or 1099-INT show foreign tax paid? You can usually take it as a credit, and without Form 1116 if it's $300 or less ($600 joint) of passive income.  
  Forms: [Form 1116](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1116), [1099-DIV](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099div)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 2, Income: interest, dividends and sales of stocks or bonds.

Terms to know: [Interest](../definitions/README.md), [Dividends](../definitions/README.md), [Qualified dividends](../definitions/README.md), [Capital gain or loss](../definitions/README.md), [Unearned income](../definitions/README.md), [Kiddie tax](../definitions/README.md), [Foreign tax credit](../definitions/README.md), [Digital assets](../definitions/README.md)

## Benefits & government payments: Did you receive any benefits?

Some government payments are taxable, some are partly taxable, and some aren't income at all.


### What you received

- Did you receive unemployment compensation? Did you repay any of it?  
  Forms: [1099-G](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099g)
- Did you get a state or local tax refund? Did you itemize deductions last year?  
  Forms: [1099-G](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099g), [Schedule 1](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1)
- Did you receive Social Security or railroad retirement benefits? Was any of it a lump sum for earlier years?  
  Forms: [SSA-1099](../form-1040/2025/index.html#ssa)
- Did you receive alimony? When was the divorce or separation agreement signed?  
  Forms: [Schedule 1](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1)
- Did you receive child support, gifts, workers' compensation or veterans' benefits? (Usually not taxable.)  
  Forms: [Schedule 1](../form-1040/2025/index.html#s1)
- Did you have gambling or lottery winnings, and do you have records of losses?  
  Forms: [W-2G](../form-1040/2025/index.html#w2g), [Schedule A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sa)

### Credits to check

- Are you retired on permanent and total disability and receiving taxable disability income? Check the credit for the elderly or disabled.  
  Forms: [Schedule R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#schr)
- Did you buy Marketplace health insurance while you were unemployed or between jobs? The premium tax credit has to be reconciled, and you may be owed more.  
  Forms: [Form 8962](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8962), [1095-A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1095a)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 2, Income: Social Security, unemployment, state refunds, alimony and gambling.

Terms to know: [Unemployment compensation](../definitions/README.md), [Social Security benefits](../definitions/README.md), [Alimony](../definitions/README.md), [Child support](../definitions/README.md), [Nontaxable income](../definitions/README.md), [Gambling winnings](../definitions/README.md), [Credit for the elderly or disabled](../definitions/README.md), [Premium tax credit (PTC)](../definitions/README.md)

## Retirement: Did you save for retirement or take money out?

Putting money in can lower your tax or earn a credit. Taking money out is usually taxable, and early withdrawals can cost an extra 10%.


### Contributions & withdrawals

- Did you contribute to a traditional or Roth IRA? Are you or your spouse covered by a retirement plan at work?  
  Forms: [IRA deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#ira), [W-2](../form-1040/2025/index.html#w2)
- Did you have earned income to base the IRA contribution on?  
  Forms: [IRA deduction](../form-1040/2025/index.html#ira)
- Have you ever made a nondeductible IRA contribution, or converted to a Roth?  
  Forms: [Form 8606](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8606)
- Did you contribute to a 401(k) or IRA and have a low or moderate income?  
  Forms: [Form 8880](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8880)
- Did you take money out of an IRA, 401(k) or pension? How old were you, and what was the money used for?  
  Forms: [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r), [Form 5329](../form-1040/2025/index.html#5329)
- Was the money rolled over into another retirement account within 60 days?  
  Forms: [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r)
- If you receive a pension, how old were you when payments started, and how much did you pay into the plan with after-tax money?  
  Forms: [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r)
- Was the withdrawal from an IRA or from a workplace plan like a 401(k)? Some exceptions to the 10% tax only apply to IRAs.  
  Forms: [Form 5329](../form-1040/2025/index.html#5329), [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r)

### Credits to check

- Is your income low or moderate? Contributions to an IRA, 401(k) or similar plan can earn the saver's credit of 10% to 50% of up to $2,000 per person.  
  Forms: [Form 8880](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8880)
- Did you take a retirement distribution during the last few years? Distributions can reduce the saver's credit.  
  Forms: [Form 8880](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8880), [1099-R](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1099r)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 2, Income: retirement income; page 3: contributions to a retirement account.

Terms to know: [Traditional IRA](../definitions/README.md), [Roth IRA](../definitions/README.md), [401(k)](../definitions/README.md), [Active participant](../definitions/README.md), [Early distribution](../definitions/README.md), [Rollover](../definitions/README.md), [Simplified Method](../definitions/README.md), [Basis](../definitions/README.md), [Saver's credit](../definitions/README.md)

## Your home: Do you own or rent your home?

Homeowners may deduct mortgage interest and property taxes if they itemize. Energy improvements and rentals have their own forms.


### Your home costs

- Do you have a mortgage? When was the loan taken out, and how much do you owe?  
  Forms: [1098](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1098), [Schedule A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sa)
- Did you buy a home or refinance this year, and did you pay points?  
  Forms: [1098](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1098)
- Did you pay real estate taxes, state income tax or sales tax, or personal property tax on a vehicle?  
  Forms: [Schedule A](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sa)
- Did you install solar panels, battery storage, a heat pump, insulation, or energy-efficient windows or doors in 2025?  
  Forms: [Form 5695](../form-1040/2025/index.html#5695)
- Do you rent out all or part of a property? How many days did you use it yourself?  
  Forms: [Schedule E](../form-1040/2025/index.html#se)
- Did you sell your home? Do you have Form 1099-S, and how long did you own and live in it?  
  Forms: [Schedule D / 8949](../form-1040/2025/index.html#sd)

### Credits to check

- Did you install solar, wind, geothermal or battery storage at a home you live in, by December 31, 2025? The residential clean energy credit is 30% of the cost.  
  Forms: [Form 5695](../form-1040/2025/index.html#5695)
- Did you add insulation, energy-efficient windows or doors, a heat pump, or have a home energy audit in 2025? The home improvement credit has annual caps.  
  Forms: [Form 5695](../form-1040/2025/index.html#5695)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 2, Income: rental income; page 3: mortgage interest, taxes and selling a home.

Terms to know: [Itemized deductions](../definitions/README.md), [Mortgage interest deduction](../definitions/README.md), [Points](../definitions/README.md), [SALT deduction](../definitions/README.md), [Residential clean energy credit](../definitions/README.md), [Energy efficient home improvement credit](../definitions/README.md)

## School & education: Did anyone in your household go to school?

College costs can earn a credit, and student loan interest can be an adjustment to income.


### The student

- Did you, your spouse or a dependent attend college or a trade school? Do you have Form 1098-T?  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)
- Is this one of the student's first four years of college? Were they enrolled at least half-time?  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)
- What did you pay for tuition, fees, books and required materials? Were any costs paid with tax-free scholarships?  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)
- Did you pay interest on a student loan?  
  Forms: [1098-E](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1098e)
- Is the student someone else's dependent?  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863), [1098-E](../form-1040/2025/index.html#1098e), [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)
- Is the student your dependent? Only the person who claims the student can take the education credit for them, and a full-time student under 24 can still be your qualifying child.  
  Forms: [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep), [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)

### Credits to check

- Is the student in the first four years of college, enrolled at least half-time, and not claimed for the AOTC in four earlier years? The American opportunity credit is up to $2,500, and 40% of it (up to $1,000) is refundable.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)
- Did the student receive a Pell grant or other scholarship? Tax-free grants reduce the expenses you can claim. If the grant's terms allow it to be used for room and board, the student can choose to include part of it in income, which frees up tuition to claim the AOTC. Compare the extra tax with the larger credit.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)
- Is the student past the first four years, part-time, or taking courses to improve job skills? The lifetime learning credit is 20% of up to $10,000 of expenses, with no limit on the number of years.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)
- Are you claiming both credits? You can't claim the AOTC and the lifetime learning credit for the same student in the same year.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)
- Are you the student, under 24, and claiming the AOTC on your own return? The refundable part may not be available to you.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863), [Dependents](../form-1040/2025/index.html#dep)
- Is your modified AGI between $80,000 and $90,000 ($160,000 and $180,000 joint)? Both education credits shrink in that range and stop above it.  
  Forms: [Form 8863](../form-1040/2025/index.html#8863)

On the IRS intake sheet: [Form 13614-C](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13614c.pdf), page 3, Expenses and Tax Related Events: student loan interest, teacher supplies and educational classes.

## Paid preparers: what the IRS requires of you

If you prepare returns for pay and a return claims the earned income credit, the child tax credit or credit for other dependents, the American opportunity credit, or head of household status, you must meet four due diligence requirements. Each failure can cost a penalty: $650 for 2025 returns ($635 for 2024 returns, $665 for 2026 returns).

1. Complete and submit Form 8867 with the return, based on information the taxpayer gave you.
1. Compute the credits correctly, and keep the worksheets that show how.
1. Meet the knowledge requirement: interview the taxpayer, and ask more questions when anything seems incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete.
1. Keep records of the checklist, worksheets, the questions you asked and the answers, and any documents you reviewed, for 3 years.

Official sources: [Form 8867](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8867.pdf), [Form 8867 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8867.pdf), [Completing Form 8867](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/completing-form-8867), [Due diligence requirements](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/due-diligence-law-regulations-and-requirements).
